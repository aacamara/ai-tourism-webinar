What is AI tourism?

61%of Virginia CPAs report some active use of AI

The reveal

Everyone in between is an AI tourist.

Source: Virginia Society of CPAs, 2026 AI Survey (n = 144 Virginia CPAs, May 26 to June 19, 2026). V2-1

Accounts are provisioned. Dashboards look healthy. Most people run one basic prompt a week and change nothing about how they work.

That is not adoption. That is tourism.

What is the same, and what is different

Same as past technologies

  • Adoption typically follows an S-curveB1-04
  • Gains lag the redesign of work (the J-curve)C-03
  • Value comes from changing workflows, not installing toolsA1-08

Different this time

  • Speed: faster than the PC or the internetB2-01
  • Bottom-up: staff adopt before the firm doesA2-46
  • Cheap to try: the cost of a GPT-3.5-level answer fell more than 280-fold in two yearsA2-41
  • Output must be checked: the work moves to the reviewerC-21

The adoption engine

Run AI like a product: a two-week loop.

The two-week loop that turns tourists into regulars

  1. Captureevery request
  2. Categorizeby workflow
  3. Prioritizeby hours returned
  4. Actship one fix
  5. Close the looptell them what changed

Close the loop and a skeptic becomes a champion. People keep using what answers them back. Six cycles is about 90 days. By then the loop runs itself.

Glossary and FAQ

Terms and common questions

Glossary

AI tourism

Access to AI tools without any change in how the work gets done. The test: if the platform disappeared tomorrow, would anyone's Monday be different?

Assembly work

Gathering before thinking: pulling files, chasing documents, tying out and reconciling. Where AI earns its keep.

Adoption curve (S-curve)

The cumulative share of people or firms using a technology over time. It rises slowly, then quickly, then levels off.

Diffusion of innovations

Everett Rogers' theory of how new ideas spread, with five adopter groups: innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority and laggards.

Bass model

A forecasting model of adoption driven by innovation (outside influence, p) and imitation (influence of earlier adopters, q).

The chasm

Geoffrey Moore's term for the gap between enthusiastic early adopters and pragmatic early-majority buyers who want proof from peers.

Hype Cycle

Gartner's model of expectations over time (peak, trough, recovery). It tracks expectations, not actual adoption.

Productivity J-curve

Measured productivity dips while firms make hard-to-measure complementary investments, then rises once they pay off.

General-purpose technology

A technology used across the economy that improves over time and spawns complementary innovations, such as electricity, computers and AI.

Bring your own AI (shadow AI)

Employees using AI tools the firm has not approved, often with firm or client data.

Jagged frontier

AI is strong on some tasks and weak on others that look similar. Work just outside the frontier can get worse with AI.

Workflow redesign

Changing how a piece of work is done, including roles and review steps, rather than adding a tool to the old process.

Champion network

A small group of trained, respected staff who help colleagues adopt AI in their own workflows.

Agentic AI

AI systems that carry out multi-step tasks with some autonomy, such as drafting, checking and filing.

IRC §7216

The Internal Revenue Code section that restricts tax return preparers from disclosing or using tax return information without consent or an exception.
